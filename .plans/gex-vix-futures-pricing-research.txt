====================================================================
1. WHY THIS PLAN EXISTS
====================================================================
The SPX research pass (gex-spx-index-options-plan.txt 5.7) found a VIX
call quoted below its spot-minus-strike intrinsic value and concluded
VIX options are priced off the VIX futures curve, so VIX was kept out
of INDEX_SYMBOLS. The human asked for a real answer to: is VIX support
feasible for this app at all, what data would it need (in particular,
is a VIX futures curve reachable without a paid feed), what model is
correct, and is it worth building

====================================================================
2. VERDICT (short version - details in section 13)
====================================================================
FEASIBLE, AND CHEAPER THAN EXPECTED - BUT NOT BY "ADDING A FUTURES FEED"

  - The futures curve IS freely reachable (Cboe publishes VX settlement
    prices as a public no-auth CSV and JSON on www.cboe.com, section
    5.3). yfinance does NOT have VX futures at all (section 5.2)
  - However, the futures feed turns out to be the WRONG primary input:
    (a) Cboe's published settlement for the WEEKLY VX futures is a
    placeholder copied from a monthly contract and disagrees with the
    market by up to 1.17 VIX points (section 5.4), and VIX weekly
    options (VIXW) are a large share of the listed expirations; (b) the
    settlement is end-of-day while the option quotes are intraday
  - The correct per-expiration forward can be read straight out of the
    option chain via put-call parity. On the 2026-10-02 snapshot the
    parity forward matches the monthly VX settlement within +-0.06 on
    every monthly expiration that has two-sided quotes, and it gives a
    sensible value for weeklies where the futures feed does not
    (section 5.5). The repo ALREADY has a parity estimator
    (src/utils.ts estimateSpot) that lands within 0.10 of the monthly
    futures on all 13 expirations (section 5.5)
  - So: no new data source, no proxy change and no Python change are
    needed for v1. The work is client-side math - a Black-76 greeks
    function, a Black-76 implied-vol solver (needed because Yahoo's
    VIX IVs are spot-based garbage, section 5.6), a per-expiration
    forward, and a VIX dispatch at the single enrichment entry point.
    CACHE, CBOE and YAHOO all work this way (CACHE included - section
    5.8)
  - The hard part is not pricing, it is GEX semantics: each VIX
    expiration has a different underlying (its own forward), so the
    current "one spot for all selected expirations" GEX math and the
    Chart tab's spot-price overlay do not carry over cleanly (section
    9). Recommended v1 scope restricts GEX for VIX to per-expiration
    use
  - Effort estimate: 3 small PRs, roughly 2-4 agent-days total
    including tests (section 12)

  Also important: VIX is NOT currently unsupported. The CBOE provider
  already routes VIX -> _VIX and the proxy suggests it, so today a user
  who types VIX on CBOE gets a chain whose higher-order greeks and GEX
  are computed off spot 15.31 (wrong). Section 4 has the details. That
  makes this work a correctness fix as much as a feature

====================================================================
  In scope: VIX (Cboe Volatility Index) options, roots VIX (monthly)
  and VIXW (weekly), on the CACHE, CBOE and YAHOO providers
  Out of scope: VXN and any other volatility-index options (same class

5.5 Put-call parity forward vs VX settlement. THE CHAIN ALREADY CONTAINS THE FORWARD
--------------------------------------------------------------

  so F = K + (C - P) / DF. Robust estimate used here (scratch script on
  the Cboe _VIX.json snapshot): only strikes where both legs have
  bid > 0, ask > 0 and spread < 0.50, take the 5 strikes with the
  smallest |C - P|, median of their F estimates, DF = exp(-0.045 T):

6. PRICING MODEL - BLACK-76 (options on futures), PRECISE FORMULAS
====================================================================

6.1 Why Black-76
--------------------------------------------------------------
  A VIX option settles to the VIX Special Opening Quotation on the same
  morning its matching VX future settles, so at expiry the option is an
  option on the future's final value. Under the risk-neutral measure the
  future is a martingale (zero drift, no carry, no dividend), so the
  standard model is Black (1976): lognormal F with volatility sigma,
  discounting at r. Section 5.5 and 5.6 show the market and Cboe both
  behave this way. Known model limitation: VIX itself is mean-reverting
  and VIX implied vols have a strong upward skew, so a single
  lognormal sigma per strike (exactly what this app already does for
  equities, one IV per contract) is the conventional approximation,
  not a perfect model. That is acceptable for this app: IV is solved per
  contract, so the skew is captured in the per-strike sigma

6.2 Formulas (match src/greeks.ts conventions: theta per calendar day,
    vega per 1 vol point, rho per 1 percentage point of r)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Inputs: F = forward for that expiration (section 7), K = strike,
  sigma = Black-76 IV (section 6.4), T = yearsToExpiration(expiration)
  (reuse the existing function, same +1 day rule), r = BS_RISK_FREE_RATE
  (0.045). No dividend yield term exists in this model

    sqrtT = sqrt(T)
    d1    = ( ln(F / K) + 0.5 * sigma^2 * T ) / ( sigma * sqrtT )
    d2    = d1 - sigma * sqrtT
    D     = exp(-r * T)              (discount factor)
    pdf   = normPdf(d1)

  Price:
    call  = D * ( F * N(d1)  - K * N(d2)  )
    put   = D * ( K * N(-d2) - F * N(-d1) )

  First order:
    delta_call = D * N(d1)
    delta_put  = D * ( N(d1) - 1 )          ( = -D * N(-d1) )
    gamma      = D * pdf / ( F * sigma * sqrtT )
    vega       = F * D * pdf * sqrtT / 100
    theta_year = -( F * D * pdf * sigma ) / ( 2 * sqrtT ) + r * V
                 where V is that option's model price (call or put)
                 (holding F fixed)
    theta      = theta_year / 365
    rho        = -T * V / 100
                 (THIS is the one greek that differs from the
                 generalized BS shortcut in 6.3: with F held fixed, r
                 only enters through D)

  Leverage:
    lambda = delta * F / optPrice   (optPrice chosen exactly as in
             blackScholesGreeks: last, else mid, else (bid+ask)/2, else
             model price)

  Higher order (all derivatives with respect to F, sigma, t):
    vanna  = -D * pdf * d2 / sigma / 100
    vomma  = (F * D * pdf * sqrtT) * d1 * d2 / sigma / 10000
    charm_call_raw = r * D * N(d1)  + D * pdf * d2 / (2 * T)
    charm_put_raw  = -r * D * N(-d1) + D * pdf * d2 / (2 * T)
    charm  = charm_raw / 365
    speed  = -gamma * ( 1 + d1 / (sigma * sqrtT) ) / F
    zomma  = gamma * ( d1 * d2 - 1 ) / sigma / 100
    color_raw = -gamma * ( 2 * r * T + 1 - d1 * d2 ) / ( 2 * T )
    color  = color_raw / 365

  Signs and scaling of charm and color follow the existing code's
  convention exactly (they are what blackScholesGreeks' own charmRaw /
  colorRaw expressions reduce to when S = F and q = r - see 6.3), so
  the UI columns stay comparable between VIX and everything else

6.3 Test oracle: Black-76 is generalized BS with S = F and q = r
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Substituting S = F and dividendYield = r into blackScholesGreeks'
  d1 gives ln(F/K) + 0.5 sigma^2 T (the r - q term vanishes), discQ
  becomes D, and every expression above falls out. Checked numerically
  for the price:

    B76 call 0.774039532958309  BS(S=F,q=r) call 0.7740395329583087

  and for delta and gamma (5.6 table: B76 delta 0.3300 gamma 0.0903 vs
  generalized-BS delta 0.33000 gamma 0.09027). So a unit test can
  assert black76Greeks(...) equals blackScholesGreeks(q, F, r, r) for
  delta, gamma, theta, vega, lambda, vanna, vomma, charm, speed, zomma,
  color, and rho === -T * price / 100. Do NOT implement Black-76 by
  calling blackScholesGreeks with q = r in production: rho would be
  wrong, and it couples the VIX path to the equity function's internals
  (the human asked for the equity and SPX path to stay untouched). Write
  a standalone function and use the identity only in tests

6.4 Implied vol solver (needed - providers' IV is not reusable)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  For each VIX quote: price P = mid if bid > 0 and ask > 0, else skip
  (do not solve on stale last trades; mark missing). Bounds a solvable
  price must satisfy:
    call: D * max(F - K, 0) < P < D * F
    put:  D * max(K - F, 0) < P < D * K
  Outside the bounds -> no IV, greeksMissingReason 'price_out_of_bounds'.
  Inside: bisection on sigma in [0.01, 10] (VIX IVs of 300%+ are real on
  near weeklies, 5.1), ~60-100 iterations or |model - P| < 1e-6;
  Black-76 price is monotone in sigma so bisection is safe and has no
  Newton-on-tiny-vega failure mode. Deep ITM options (Cboe iv 0.0)
  will often be at or below the lower bound after rounding - expect and
  accept missing IV there, same as Cboe

7. FORWARD SELECTION RULE (per expiration)
====================================================================
7.1 Rule
--------------------------------------------------------------
  For each expiration E in the VIX chain:
    1. Collect strikes where both call and put have bid > 0 and ask > 0
       and each spread (ask - bid) <= max(0.50, 25% of mid)
    2. For each, Fk = K + (Cmid - Pmid) / D   with D = exp(-r * T(E))
    3. Take the 3-5 strikes with the smallest |Cmid - Pmid| and use the
       MEDIAN of their Fk as F(E)
    4. If step 1 yields no strikes: fall back to the existing
       estimateSpot(quotes, E) (mid, else last trade) and mark the
       expiration's quotes greeksSource 'black-76' with a low-confidence
       flag (see 8.2); if that also returns null, leave the expiration's
       greeks empty with greeksMissingReason 'missing_forward'
  Never substitute the spot index (underlyingPrice) for F(E) for VIX,
  and never use a weekly VX settlement (5.4)

7.2 Why not just reuse estimateSpot as-is
--------------------------------------------------------------
  It already works well (5.5), but it (a) uses last trade when bid or
  ask is 0, which on sparse weeklies can be stale by hours or days, and
  (b) trusts a single strike. The median-of-near-ATM version is a small
  new function; keep estimateSpot unchanged for its existing callers

7.3 Optional futures cross-check (Phase 4, not v1)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Monthly VX settlements from 5.3(a) could be shown as a reference ("VX
  Oct fut 17.648") and used as a fallback F for MONTHLY expirations only
  when 7.1 fails. This requires a new proxy route because www.cboe.com
  sends no CORS header. Only worth doing if 7.1 is found to fail often
  in practice

9. GEX AND CHART IMPLICATIONS (the genuinely hard part)
====================================================================
  - gexCall (src/gex.ts:34) = gamma * OI * 100 * spot^2 * 0.01, with ONE
    spot for every selected expiration. For VIX, gamma is with respect
    to F(E), which differs per expiration (16.48 to 20.88 on the
    snapshot, 5.5). Using spot 15.31 understates GEX by (15.31 /
    17.67)^2 = 0.75x on the Oct monthly and 0.54x on the Jun-27
    monthly, and the gamma itself is with respect to a different
    underlying
  - Summing GEX across VIX expirations assumes a 1% move in spot VIX
    moves every future by 1%. It does not - VX futures have a beta to
    spot well below 1 that falls with tenor (term structure). Summing
    is therefore not meaningful the way it is for SPX
  - The GEX x-axis and walls are in strike space. For VIX, strike space
    is futures space, so walls and gamma flip are levels of the
    matching-month future, not of spot VIX. The Chart tab draws GEX
    levels on the ^VIX SPOT price chart (use-gex-levels.ts feeds both
    tabs), which would place them ~2-5 points off where they belong

  Recommended v1 behavior for VIX (all small, UI-level):
    1. computeGexProfile: use q.forward ?? spot per quote in place of
       the single spot when q.forward is present (one-line change inside
       the loop, no change for quotes without forward - equity and SPX
       behavior identical)
    2. useGexLevels: when the symbol is futures-priced, use F(E) of the
       nearest selected expiration as the reference "spot" (instead of
       underlyingPrice) and show a note "VIX levels are in <month>
       futures terms (F = 17.67), not spot VIX (15.31)"
    3. When more than one VIX expiration is selected, show a warning
       that cross-expiration aggregation mixes different underlyings
       (or restrict the VIX GEX view to a single expiration - pick one
       at implementation time and document it in the view)
    4. Chart tab: for VIX, do not draw GEX price lines on the spot chart
       (or draw them with an explicit "futures level" label). Hiding is
       the safer default
  These are presentation decisions; ask the human if unsure which of
  3's two options to take

13. RECOMMENDATION (Q5)
====================================================================
  BUILD IT, using the chain-implied forward, not a futures feed.

  Why it is worth it:
  - The app already half-serves VIX on CBOE with wrong numbers, so the
    status quo is not "unsupported", it is "misleading" (section 4)
  - The model is standard and fully specified (section 6), and the
    whole thing is client-side and testable against an exact identity
    (6.3)
  - No new upstream, no new proxy route, no Python change and no schema
    change. CACHE, CBOE and YAHOO all get support (5.8)
  - The free futures feed exists (5.3), so if the parity forward ever
    proves unreliable there is a no-cost fallback for monthlies (7.3)

  What makes it less than trivial:
  - GEX semantics across expirations (section 9) - this is a product
    question as much as a math one, and is where most of the effort
    goes
  - Weekly VIX options: neither the futures feed nor Cboe's greeks can
    be trusted for them (5.4); the parity forward is the only sound
    input, and on very sparse weeklies even that can fall back to stale
    last-trade prices (5.5, 7.1 step 4)

  What NOT to do: add VIX to INDEX_SYMBOLS (applies spot pricing AND an
  S&P dividend yield), or build v1 around the VX settlement CSV (wrong
  for weeklies, end-of-day only, needs CORS proxying)

14. EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE (do not build these unasked)
====================================================================
  - VXN and other volatility-index options - same problem class, not
    tested. Revisit with the same section 5 checks if wanted
  - Showing VX futures as their own chain or ticker
  - A stochastic-vol or mean-reverting VIX model (e.g. Grunbichler-
    Longstaff, Whaley-style VIX models) - per-contract Black-76 IV is
    the market convention and matches what Cboe publishes for monthlies
  - Any data/options/*.json schema change or any Black-76 math in
    scripts/options-data.py (the hard rule that model math lives only
    client-side applies unchanged)

15. LIMITATIONS AND ADDITIONAL RESEARCH REQUIRED (read this - honest)
====================================================================
  1. ONE SNAPSHOT. Every number comes from the 2026-10-02 close, read on
     a Sunday. The parity-vs-futures agreement (5.5) was observed on one
     day in a calm, contango market (spot 15.31). It was not tested in
     backwardation (VIX spike) when weekly and front-month dynamics are
     more extreme. Re-run the 5.5 script on a stressed day before
     relying on the +-0.06 figure as typical
  2. INTRADAY NOT OBSERVED. The get_quotes_combined JSON (5.3(b)) showed
     last_price 0.0 because markets were closed. Whether it carries
     live delayed prices during trading hours, and how stale Cboe's
     delayed option quotes are relative to it, was not checked. This
     matters only for the optional Phase 4
  3. CBOE WEEKLY SETTLEMENT RULE NOT FOUND. Section 5.4 shows the weekly
     VX settlements copy a monthly value on two dates, but the official
     Cboe rule for settling untraded weekly VX futures was not located
     or read. The conclusion "do not use weekly settlements" stands on
     the empirical mismatch with the option market regardless, but the
     exact mechanism is unverified
  4. OTHER FREE FUTURES SOURCES NOT EXHAUSTIVELY SEARCHED. Only Yahoo
     (yfinance) and Cboe endpoints were probed. Terms of use for
     scripted access to www.cboe.com/us/futures/... were not reviewed;
     moot for v1 (not used) but must be checked before Phase 4
  5. DAY COUNT AND RATE. The scratch scripts used the repo's
     yearsToExpiration rule (+1 calendar day) and r = 0.045. VIX options
     settle at the morning SOQ, so the true time to expiry is about half
     a day less than a close-to-close count; on 5-day weeklies that
     moves greeks noticeably. The residuals vs Cboe in 5.6 (delta within
     ~0.005, gamma within ~0.002) are consistent with day-count and
     forward-timing differences but were not decomposed. Matching the
     existing convention keeps VIX consistent with the rest of the app;
     an intraday time-to-expiry is a possible later refinement for all
     symbols, not VIX-specific
  6. GEX SEMANTICS ARE A JUDGMENT CALL. Section 9's per-quote-forward
     scaling is defensible, but there is no public standard for VIX GEX,
     and dealer hedging of VIX options happens in VX futures. Treat VIX
     GEX levels as more approximate than SPX ones and say so in the UI
  7. SPARSE WEEKLIES. 11-04 had zero bids on most strikes, so the strict
     parity rule failed and only the last-trade fallback produced a
     forward. How often that happens, and whether such expirations
     should simply be hidden for VIX, was not measured across days
  8. SIDE NOTE, NOT VIX-SPECIFIC: on 2026-10-04 cdn.cboe.com answered
     the options endpoint (both _VIX.json and _SPX.json) with HTTP 307
     -> cdn-api.cboe.com. curl -L, fetch() and requests follow
     redirects by default, so the existing proxies and options-data.py
     likely still work, but this was not re-verified through the actual
     proxies in this pass. Worth a quick check by whoever touches the
     CBOE provider next
